## Legal Background

### California — Assembly Bill 1043 (“Digital Age Assurance Act”)

California has enacted **AB 1043**, effective January 1, 2027, which requires:

- Operating system providers to prompt account holders for the **user’s birth date or age** at account setup.  
- Applications to **request the age-bracket signal via an API** when downloaded or launched.  
- Developers that receive the signal to treat it as **actual knowledge** of the user’s age bracket and comply with related legal obligations.  

This software **does not implement any mechanism to request or process such age signals** and therefore **cannot be used by residents of California** without violating the law. ([calmatters.digitaldemocracy.org](https://calmatters.digitaldemocracy.org/bills/ca_202520260ab1043?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

### Colorado — Senate Bill 26‑051 (“Age Attestation on Computing Devices”)

Colorado SB26‑051 requires a similar framework:

- Operating systems must collect a **user’s birth date or age** at account setup.  
- Applications must **request the age signal via an API** when downloaded or launched.  
- Developers that receive the signal are deemed to **know the user’s age** and must act accordingly.  

This software does **not** implement these mechanisms and **cannot be used by residents of Colorado** without violating the law. ([leg.colorado.gov](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/sb26-051?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

## Project Rationale

The maintainers intentionally **avoid collecting personal information**, including age or birth date. Implementing age-signal requests as required under AB 1043 or SB26‑051 would require:

- Integrating with operating system APIs for age signals,  
- Handling personal or sensitive information,  
- Significant development and compliance work outside the scope of this project.  

Due to these constraints, and to **avoid legal risk**, the software **cannot be used in California or Colorado**.
