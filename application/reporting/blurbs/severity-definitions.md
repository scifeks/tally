## Severity Definitions

The following severity tiers are used throughout this report.  Scores are
aligned with the Common Vulnerability Scoring System version 3.1 (CVSS 3.1).

**Critical** (CVSS 9.0–10.0)
A critical finding represents an immediately exploitable vulnerability that
could result in full system or data compromise with little or no user
interaction.  Remediation should be treated as an emergency and addressed
within 24–48 hours of disclosure.

**High** (CVSS 7.0–8.9)
A high-severity finding describes a significant vulnerability that is likely
exploitable by an attacker with standard capabilities and could result in
substantial data loss, service disruption, or privilege escalation.
Remediation should be prioritised and completed within two weeks of
disclosure.

**Medium** (CVSS 4.0–6.9)
A medium-severity finding represents a vulnerability that is exploitable under
certain conditions or requires some degree of user interaction.  The potential
impact is limited in scope but should not be ignored.  Remediation should be
planned and completed within 30 days of disclosure.

**Low** (CVSS 0.1–3.9)
A low-severity finding indicates a weakness with limited exploitability or
minimal impact in isolation.  These findings often require chaining with other
vulnerabilities to be effective.  Remediation should be addressed during the
next scheduled maintenance or development cycle.

**Informational** (CVSS 0.0)
An informational finding does not represent a direct security risk but
highlights a configuration, practice, or observation that could contribute to
risk over time or represent a deviation from security best practice.  These
findings should be reviewed and addressed at the organisation's discretion.
